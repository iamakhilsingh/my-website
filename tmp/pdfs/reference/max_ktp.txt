Introduction
This document provides an overview of kidney transplantation in India
(Max Super Speciality Hospital, Saket) intended for patients and their
families.
It covers recipient and donor evaluation, immunological testing, legal
framework under NOTTO and surgical techniques, post-transplant care, and
transplant program workflow with emphasis on practice in Delhi, India.

--- PAGE 3 ---
Indications for Kidney Transplant
• Chronic kidney disease stage 5 (eGFR <15 ml/min)
• Dialysis-dependent renal failure
Types of Kidney Transplant
• Living-related transplant – robotic/open kidney transplant
• ABO incompatible transplant
• Paired kidney exchange transplant (Swap)
• Deceased donor transplant
• Paediatric Kidney Transplant

Donor Selection Criteria
• First and second degree relative
• Age typically between 18 and 65 years
• Normal kidney function
• No uncontrolled hypertension
• No diabetes
• No chronic disease
• The donor must have the same blood group or blood group O+. In cases
where this is not feasible, an ABO-incompatible kidney transplant may be
considered.
• An AB+ patient can receive a kidney from any blood group.

--- PAGE 7 ---
Authorisation Committee Process
• Authorisation committee approval is required for all.
• The committee verifies relationship proof, financial background, and voluntary
consent.
Surgical Technique – Recipient Operation
• Robotic or Open Surgery
• The donor kidney is placed in the
iliac fossa
• The renal artery is anastomosed to
external or internal iliac artery
• Renal vein anastomosed to external
iliac vein
• Ureter implanted into bladder using
ureteroneocystostomy
Donor Surgery
Laparoscopic donor nephrectomy is the preferred technique.
Advantages
Less post-operative pain
Faster recovery
Shorter hospital stay
Immunosuppressive Therapy
Induction Therapy
• Grafalon
• Antithymocyte globulin
• Rituximab ABO-incompatible transplant
Maintenance Therapy
• Tacrolimus
• Mycophenolate mofetil
• Methylprednisolone / Wysolone

Post Transplant Monitoring
Serum creatinine Serum potassium
Tacrolimus levels Urine analysis
Random blood sugar Blood pressure monitoring
Infection Prophylaxis
• Cotrimoxazole for Pneumocystis prophylaxis
• Valganciclovir for CMV prophylaxis
• Antifungal prophylaxis in selected patients
Follow-up Protocol
• First month: 2 visits per week
• First three months: 1 visit per week
• After six months: fortnightly visits
Outcomes
• One-year graft survival for living donor transplant is approximately 95-97%.
• Long-term success depends on medication adherence and regular follow-up.
Lifestyle After Transplant
• Avoid infection exposure
• Maintain a healthy diet
• Regular exercise
• Avoid nephrotoxic medicines

Legal Requirements
Documents required for donor and recipient International Patient
• Passport
• Medical visa
• National ID
• Birth certificate or marriage certificate
• Family photos with donor and recipient (10–12)
• Family consent affidavit with notary
• 12 passport-sized photographs
• Medical and psychological evaluation of donor
• Approval from government-authorised hospital committee
Important Note
Patients are advised to connect with Max’s International and Legal Team before
travelling to verify all legal documentation. The following details must be shared
in advance along with the required documents:
• Age of the patient
• Age of the donor
• Blood group of the patient
• Blood group of the donor
• Profession of both the patient and the donor
• Relationship between the patient and the donor
• If the donor is the patient’s spouse, please provide the duration
of marriage, number of children, and their ages

Financial and Administrative Requirements
• Passport copies of patient and accompanying attendant/carer for issuance
of medical visa invitation letter.
• We have a currency exchange service available within the hospital premises.
The International Patient Department will be pleased to assist you with the
same. Alternatively, payments may be made through wire transfer, credit
card, or cash upon arrival.
Please note: Each passport holder is permitted to use a maximum of USD
4,900 in cash toward hospital bill payments and may make payments in INR up
to `25,000 only
Visa Rules
• The International Patient Services (IPS) team assists patients in obtaining a
visa by issuing a support letter addressed to the Indian High Commission in
the patient’s home country. This facilitates the issuance of a Medical Visa for
both the patient and accompanying attendants.
• The IPS team also assists in arranging visa extension letters in cases
where the patient requires a longer stay in India for continued treatment or
recovery.
• Please note: As per the latest FRRO regulations, patients requiring inpatient
treatment or hospital admission are advised to travel on a valid Medical
Visa. Patients arriving on a Tourist Visa may only be eligible for OPD
(outpatient) consultations and services.
Accommodation Support
• The hospital’s international patient services team can assist in arranging
accommodation near the hospital for patients and attendants depending on
the budget and duration of stay.
